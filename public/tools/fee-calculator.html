How the math works

Both platforms now take one fee out of your payout, so whatever price you set, you take home a fixed share of it. Here are the same three questions for each platform: what if you do nothing, what if you want your old payout back, and what if you want your old listed price to become your new payout.

Airbnb: 15.5% host-paid fee, starting September 15, 2026

Airbnb keeps 15.5% of your price, so you take home 84.5%: payout = price × 0.845. Under the old split-fee structure you kept 97% of your listed price.

  • Do nothing: your price stays the same and your payout becomes old price × 0.845.
  • Keep my old payout the same: new price = (old price × 0.97) ÷ 0.845, which is about 14.79% higher than your old price.
  • Keep my old price as my new payout: new price = old price ÷ 0.845, which is about 18.34% higher.

Vrbo: single 12% commission, starting October 29, 2026

Vrbo keeps 12% of the rental amount plus fees like cleaning and pet fees, so you take home 88%: payout = price × 0.88. The new rate applies to bookings made on or after October 29, 2026; earlier bookings keep their original fee. Traveler service fees are separate and not part of this math.

What you paid until now depends on your setup: about 5% if you connect through a property management system like Hospitable or Guesty (you kept 95%), or about 8% if you don't (a 5% commission plus 3% payment processing, so you kept 92%).

  • Do nothing: your price stays the same and your payout becomes old price × 0.88.
  • Keep my old payout the same: new price = (old price × 0.95) ÷ 0.88 starting from 5% (about 7.95% higher), or (old price × 0.92) ÷ 0.88 starting from 8% (about 4.55% higher).
  • Keep my old price as my new payout: new price = old price ÷ 0.88, which is about 13.64% higher.
